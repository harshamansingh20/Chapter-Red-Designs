A beloved Punjabi restaurant in Siliguri reintroduces itself to a new generation

ABOUT THE
BRAND

Punjabi Kadhai is one of the best Punjabi restaurants in Siliguri, West Bengal, founded by Jaspal Bindra, a Siliguri-based foodpreneur, to bring something the city had been missing for years: authentic Punjabi food. While North Indians longed for the taste of home, Bengalis struggled to find a version of Punjabi cuisine that felt genuine.

Between roadside dhaba stereotypes and commercialised chains, the rich food and heartfelt hospitality of Punjab had quietly disappeared.

Alongside his successful food ventures like Cafe Karma Diaries and Baking Diaries, Jaspal created a North Indian restaurant in Siliguri that celebrates Punjab the way it truly is. His vision was to create no exaggerated cultural clichés but only serving timeless recipes, old Punjab-inspired interiors, and the warmth that has always defined Punjabi hospitality, where every guest is welcomed like family and every meal ends with an affectionate, "Ik hor, please?"

Today, Punjabi Kadhai is a trusted destination for authentic Punjabi cuisine in Siliguri, bringing together Punjabis seeking the comfort of home, Bengalis discovering real North Indian flavours, and everyone who believes good food is best served with genuine warmth.

FINDING BRAND'S EDGE

WHAT WE FOUND

Punjab is often reduced to loud stereotypes in north indian restaurant branding: "bright colours, trucks, and exaggerated motifs". But the real Punjab is far richer. Chapter Red team uncovered an opportunity to celebrate Punjab beyond stereotypes, creating a brand rooted in culture rather than caricature. So, for a restaurant rooted in the founder's love for his heritage, the brand identity needed to honour that legacy with authenticity rather than imitation.

WHAT WE CHOSE

We built a Punjabi restaurant brand identity inspired by the quieter side of Punjab. Drawing from old Punjabi homes, timeless hospitality, handcrafted Phulkari details, and the feeling of gathering around a shared meal, every element was designed to celebrate culture through warmth, connection, and authenticity.

WHAT WE REFUSED

We deliberately stayed away from the clichés that often define Punjabi restaurant branding like loud cartoon mascots, oversized moustaches, trucks, bhangra imagery, and loud visual gimmicks. Instead of borrowing symbols, we created a timeless restaurant brand identity that reflects the true spirit of Punjab, which is welcoming, soulful, and proudly authentic.

HUMANISING
THE BRAND

If Punjabi Kadhai were a person, it would be that favourite Punjabi aunty everyone secretly hopes invites them over for dinner. The one who keeps asking, "Bas? Hor lao na!" before your plate is even empty. The one who tells stories while serving food, remembers what you liked the last time, and believes the best conversations always happen around the dining table. She of course is proud of where she comes from, though never loud about it. Her food and hospitality do all the talking.

That personality became the foundation of the brand. Through Chapter Red's strategy-first branding process, we translated these human qualities into Punjabi Kadhai's tone of voice, communication style, and visual identity, ensuring every interaction feels generous, familiar and home coming.

CRAFTING THE
IDENTITY

We didn't want people to recognise Punjab. We wanted them to feel it through its visual identity.

With the strategy and personality clearly defined, we created the visual identity which draws inspiration from old Punjabi homes, handcrafted Phulkari embroidery, and the warmth of gathering around a shared meal. Rather than relying on familiar cultural clichés, we built a visual language that feels timeless, and elegant, honouring Punjab to its true authenticity.

From the logo and typography to the packaging, menu design, signage, and supporting graphics, each touchpoint reinforces the same feeling: warm, welcoming, and proudly Punjabi. The result is a visual identity that feels distinctive, memorable, and built to grow with the business.

Together, everything all together doesn't just imitate Punjabi culture, it honours it!
